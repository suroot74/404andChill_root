VHS Rentals: Rewinding the Magic of Friday Nights

A nostalgic dive into the rituals and joys of VHS rentals, from scouring video store aisles to the sacred rule of rewinding.

By WM. Carty
4 min read
VHS Rentals: Rewinding the Magic of Friday Nights
VHS Rentals: Rewinding the Magic of Friday Nights

Friday Nights Were an Event

Before streaming queues and autoplay, Friday nights were about commitment. In the 1980s and ’90s, there was only one way to start the weekend: a trip to the video rental store. It wasn’t just a chore - it was a tradition. You’d gather the family or friends, pile into the car, and head to that fluorescent-lit temple of entertainment, ready to find the perfect movie for the night.

For my family, the ritual began after dinner. The excitement built as we walked through the door, greeted by shelves upon shelves of VHS tapes, each promising an adventure. New releases, cult classics, and hidden gems sat side by side, waiting for someone to pluck them off the shelf. The hum of the lights mixed with the faint smell of plastic cases and popcorn from the concession stand. It wasn’t just a store - it was a portal to another world.

The Art of Choosing

Deciding on a movie wasn’t a casual affair - it was a full-blown process. There was no “scroll and sample” safety net like today. This was a one-shot deal. Once you rented that tape, you were committed. My siblings and I would fan out across the store, each lobbying for our pick. Action movies? Comedies? Maybe a horror flick, if we thought we could convince Mom it wasn’t too scary. The debates were epic, each of us wielding box covers and plot summaries like evidence in a courtroom.

Every VHS case was a tiny treasure chest. The front cover had to catch your eye, but the back? That’s where the real magic happened. It was where you discovered the plot, the runtime, and, if you were lucky, a couple of grainy stills from the movie. We scrutinized each one, hunting for that perfect blend of excitement, fun, and re-watchability.

By the time we reached a decision, it felt like we’d achieved something monumental. Carrying that plastic clamshell case to the counter was like hoisting a trophy. The night was officially underway.

The Unwritten Rules of VHS

Video rental culture came with its own set of rules. Rule number one? Always check the tape before leaving the store. There was nothing worse than getting home, settling in with popcorn, and discovering the wrong movie - or worse, a blank tape. Rule number two? “Be Kind, Rewind.” Those three words weren’t just a suggestion; they were a moral imperative. Returning an un-rewound tape was practically a crime against humanity.

Then there were the late fees. Oh, the late fees. Missing a return deadline by even a day could double the cost of your rental. My dad would grumble about them every time, but it didn’t stop us from cutting it close more often than we’d admit. Yet, those rules added to the experience. They gave weight to every decision, every rental, every rewind. It wasn’t just a transaction - it was a responsibility.

The Disappearance of the Video Store

By the mid-2000s, video rental stores started vanishing. DVDs were taking over, Netflix was mailing discs to your door, and streaming services loomed on the horizon. Blockbuster, once the king of Friday nights, became a cautionary tale, and mom-and-pop stores struggled to survive. The aisles of VHS tapes were replaced by digital catalogs, and the tactile joy of browsing turned into endless scrolling.

Don’t get me wrong - streaming is convenient. But convenience doesn’t always come with the same magic. Renting a movie was about more than watching - it was about the journey. The anticipation. The little moments, like stumbling across a hidden gem or the thrill of snagging the last copy of a hot new release. Digital menus can’t replicate the feeling of holding a VHS case in your hands, weighing its potential like a precious artifact.

What We Lost and What We Remember

Looking back, I wonder what today’s kids will reminisce about when they think of movie nights. Will they remember the excitement of the hunt, the joy of discovery? Or will it all blur into a haze of algorithms and autoplay recommendations?

For those of us who grew up in the VHS era, the memories are vivid: the debates, the rituals, the magic of pressing play and hearing the tape whir to life. It wasn’t just about the movies - it was about the experience. And even though those days are gone, their echoes remain. What would you pick if you could step back into those aisles? Would it be an action-packed blockbuster or a feel-good family flick? Let’s rewind and relive the magic together.
