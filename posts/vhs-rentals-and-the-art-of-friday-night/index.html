VHS Rentals and the Art of Friday Night

A nostalgic dive into the ritual of VHS rentals, from scouring the aisles to rewinding tapes before return - a lost art from a simpler time.

By WM. Carty
3 min read
VHS Rentals and the Art of Friday Night
VHS Rentals and the Art of Friday Night

Friday Nights Were an Event

There was a time when Friday nights didn’t revolve around endless scrolling through streaming services. No, back in the 1980s and ’90s, Friday night meant one thing: a trip to the video rental store. It wasn’t just an errand - it was a ritual, a pilgrimage to those hallowed aisles of Blockbuster, Hollywood Video, or the mom-and-pop shop tucked into a strip mall.

For my family, it was a weekly tradition. After dinner, we’d pile into the car and head to our local video store, already buzzing with anticipation. Walking through those doors felt like stepping into a treasure trove of possibilities. Rows and rows of VHS tapes lined the shelves, their covers promising everything from edge-of-your-seat thrills to belly laughs. The fluorescent lights hummed overhead, and the faint smell of plastic cases mixed with popcorn from the concession stand. It was heaven - or as close as a kid could get.

The Art of Choosing

Picking a movie was no small task. You had to commit, because this wasn’t streaming - you couldn’t change your mind 15 minutes in and pick something else. My siblings and I would split up, scouring different sections in search of the perfect pick. Action? Comedy? Horror, if we could sneak it past Mom? Every cover was a mini sales pitch, and we judged them harshly, flipping boxes over to read plot summaries and scanning for those magic words: “New Release.”

And then there were the debates. Oh, the debates. One of us wanted *Terminator 2*, another wanted *Honey, I Shrunk the Kids*. My dad would chime in with some random Western, and before you knew it, we were embroiled in full-on negotiations that could rival the UN. But somehow, we’d always settle on something, and that little plastic clamshell case would feel like a trophy as we carried it to the counter.

The Unwritten Rules of VHS

There were rules to renting, of course. First, you always checked the tape before you left the store. Was it rewound? Was it the right movie? (The horror of getting home and finding a random infomercial instead of *Jurassic Park* still haunts me.) Then came the sacred duty of rewinding before you returned it. “Be Kind, Rewind” wasn’t just a slogan - it was a social contract. Nobody wanted to be the jerk who left the tape at the end credits for the next person.

And let’s not forget the late fees. Oh, those dreaded late fees. Returning a movie a day late could cost more than the rental itself, and my dad never let us hear the end of it when we slipped up. But somehow, even the stress of returning tapes on time became part of the charm.

The Disappearance of the Video Store

By the mid-2000s, video stores started to disappear, casualties of DVDs, Netflix, and eventually, streaming. Blockbuster went from a booming giant to a fading memory, and the smaller shops didn’t stand a chance. Suddenly, the weekly ritual of browsing shelves and arguing over movie picks was gone, replaced by the convenience of sitting on your couch and clicking a button.

Sure, streaming is easier. But something got lost in the transition. Scrolling through a digital catalog just doesn’t have the same thrill as physically holding a tape in your hands, debating its merits under the fluorescent glow of a rental shop. Renting a movie wasn’t just about the movie - it was about the experience, the process, the little moments that made it feel special.

What We Lost and What We Remember

Sometimes I think about those Friday nights and wonder what today’s kids will remember about their movie-watching rituals. Will they feel the same sense of excitement, the same joy in the hunt, or will it all blur into a haze of convenience and algorithms?

For those of us who grew up in the VHS era, the memories are clear: the arguments, the anticipation, the magic of finally pressing play. It wasn’t perfect, but it was ours. And maybe, just maybe, that’s why it’s so fun to look back on it now. What movie would you choose if you could walk those aisles again?
