The Mixtape: The Underrated Genius of Britpop

Dive into Britpop: the genre that brought us Blur, Oasis, and the ultimate British rivalry of the '90s.

By WM. Carty
4 min read
The Mixtape: The Underrated Genius of Britpop
The Mixtape: The Underrated Genius of Britpop

Britpop wasn’t just a music genre - it was a movement, a vibe, a cultural phenomenon that defined the 90s on the other side of the Atlantic. While grunge was making flannel the uniform of disillusioned youth in the U.S., Britpop countered with a cocky smirk, skinny ties, and more eyeliner than an entire season of RuPaul’s Drag Race. It was unapologetically British, combining jangly guitars, sharp lyrics, and nostalgia for a Britain that likely existed only in myth. And yet, it worked brilliantly.

The Kings of Britpop: Blur vs Oasis

At the heart of Britpop was one of the most entertaining rivalries in music history: Blur vs Oasis. These weren’t just bands - they were the avatars of a cultural divide. Blur, with their art school roots and tongue-in-cheek social commentary, embodied the south of England’s wit and whimsy. Songs like Parklife and Girls & Boys turned mundane moments into bouncy, ironic anthems. Meanwhile, Oasis swaggered in from the north with unpolished confidence, churning out stadium-ready hits like Wonderwall and Don’t Look Back in Anger that seemed destined to echo through pub karaoke nights for eternity.

Their feud reached a fever pitch in 1995 when Blur’s Country House went head-to-head with Oasis’s Roll With It in what the British press dramatically dubbed “The Battle of Britpop.” Blur may have claimed the chart victory, but Oasis won the cultural war, becoming the poster children for Britpop excess. From Noel and Liam Gallagher’s tabloid-worthy brawls to their record-breaking gigs at Knebworth, Oasis defined the genre’s larger-than-life ambitions and pitfalls.

It wasn’t just about music; it was about identity. Blur’s smirk and Oasis’s snarl represented two sides of a shared cultural coin, and fans loved picking a side. (For the record: I was Team Pulp, but more on them later.)

The Other Stars in the Britpop Galaxy

While Blur and Oasis dominated the spotlight, Britpop’s universe was brimming with talent. Pulp brought a uniquely theatrical flair to the scene, with Jarvis Cocker’s wry observations on British life turning tracks like Common People into modern anthems of class commentary. Their album Different Class wasn’t just music - it was sociology you could dance to.

Then there was Suede, who fused glam rock with introspective lyrics to create something sultry and shadowy. Brett Anderson’s androgynous charisma and songs like Animal Nitrate gave Britpop a dark, sexy edge that stood out against the genre’s often cheeky exuberance. And let’s not forget Elastica, whose punchy, minimalist tracks like Connection managed to pack more attitude into two minutes than most bands could fit into an entire album.

These bands - and so many others - added depth and diversity to Britpop’s sound, proving it was more than just Blur vs Oasis. Each brought their own flavor, ensuring the movement was as eclectic as it was iconic.

Britpop’s Identity Crisis

Britpop wasn’t just music; it was a reaction to its time. The genre celebrated “Britishness” with a mix of pride and irony, leaning into the Union Jack aesthetic while poking fun at the clichés it represented. This was the era of Cool Britannia, when bands shared magazine covers with politicians, and Tony Blair cozied up to pop stars to signal his modern, youthful appeal.

But Britpop’s confidence had a flip side. By the late ’90s, its cheeky swagger began to feel more like arrogance. Blur and Oasis started to experiment with new sounds, and audiences turned their attention to the rise of electronic music and the boy band phenomenon. The very qualities that made Britpop brilliant - its bravado, its sense of place - became its limitations, leading to a slow but inevitable fade from the mainstream.

Why Britpop Still Matters

Despite its implosion, Britpop’s legacy endures. For one, it was just plain fun. At its peak, the genre reminded us that music didn’t have to be dour or overthought. It could be sharp, infectious, and unapologetically playful. Britpop also gave us some of the defining albums of the ’90s, from Blur’s Parklife to Oasis’s (What’s the Story) Morning Glory? to Pulp’s Different Class.

Modern bands like Arctic Monkeys and The 1975 owe a debt to Britpop’s blend of wit, charm, and occasional self-indulgence. Even the Blur vs Oasis rivalry lives on in meme form, with Noel and Liam Gallagher’s feuds providing endless fodder for the internet. Britpop may have been a moment, but its echoes continue to shape music and culture today.

5-Song Britpop Mixtape

Revisit the glory days of Britpop with these essential tracks:

  • Parklife - Blur
    The quintessential Britpop anthem, filled with wit, charm, and a love of the mundane.
  • Don’t Look Back in Anger - Oasis
    A stadium-sized singalong that’s become an enduring symbol of Britpop’s emotional core.
  • Common People - Pulp
    A sharp, poignant, and utterly danceable ode to class and aspiration.
  • Animal Nitrate - Suede
    Dark, glamorous, and impossible to ignore - Suede at their best.
  • Connection - Elastica
    A short, sharp burst of Britpop brilliance that encapsulates the genre’s punky edge.

Turn up the volume, throw on your best mod jacket, and let the Britpop vibes take over. After all, a little swagger and wit never go out of style.
